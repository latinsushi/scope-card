Out of scope clause, freelance

An out-of-scope clause is a short list of what the fee does not cover. It sits next to what's included. Together they are the wall.

It is not a contract lecture. It is a card. One page. Send it with the proposal, not after the extra ask.

Why "not included" beats a long contract

Independent designers, developers, and consultants on fixed-price work lose money at the boundary. A 12-page SOW does not get read. A list of six outs does.

  • Name the usual extras for your craft. Photos. Copy. Extra pages. Training.
  • Name how extras get billed. An hourly rate is enough.
  • Say work on extras starts after written approval. A reply counts.

What to put in the "not included" list

Write the things clients assume. If you would not have taken this fee with that item inside it, it belongs on the out list.

  • Design: photography, illustration, print, social templates, extra inner pages.
  • Development: third-party integrations, content entry, training, hosting, SEO campaigns.
  • Consulting: extra workshops, extra stakeholders, implementation, ongoing retainers.

Be specific. "Misc extras" is not a wall. "Photography and retouching" is.

When to send it

With the proposal. Not after they say "I thought that was included." The card is for before. After the extra ask, you need a change request, not a new list of outs.
