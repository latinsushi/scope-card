Revision rounds
This proposal includes [N] revision rounds. A revision is a change to work already listed in this proposal: copy, color, layout, or notes on something we already made. Extra rounds beyond [N], and any new pages, platforms, lockups, or features, are extra work.

Extra rounds and extra work are billed at $[rate]/hour, or at a flat fee we agree in writing. Extra rounds start only after your written approval. A reply to email is enough.

If a request is a new thing, not a revision of listed work, I will say so and send a price before I start.

Why the cap has to be a number

Independent designers, developers, and consultants lose money when "revisions included" has no count. Unlimited rounds is not a kindness. It is an open tab. Two rounds is a common default for small fixed-price jobs. Three is fine if the fee already paid for the third. Zero is a problem. Name the number they can point at in week four.

This paragraph sits under the fee, in the proposal they actually read. It is not a 12-page appendix. It is not a contract lecture. It is a business line for small fixed-price jobs. Not legal advice. A lawyer can still write a master services agreement if the job is large.

What a round is, in one sentence

A revision round is polish on listed work. Color, type, copy on a page you already designed. The layout they already approved, with notes. It is not a new page. It is not a second platform. It is not "revise the about page into a shop." If you would not have taken the original fee with this inside it, it is extra. Write that test into the proposal so you are not inventing it in Slack.

Name the extra rate on the same page. $125 or $175 invented under pressure is how extras get cheap. A named hourly or a flat extra-round fee is a wall. Name that a reply counts as approval so you are not waiting on a wet signature for a $400 extra round.

Where to put it

Under the fee. Next to the deliverables. Independent designers lose the cap when it lives on page nine of a PDF they file. The client fills the edges in because nothing at the price said "two rounds."

Paste the paragraph. Then attach a one-page card they can forward to a partner. The card is the thing that gets shown in a meeting. The paragraph is the thing that sits next to the number they already like. Use both if you can. If you only have time for one thing tonight, put the cap under the fee and send the card tomorrow.

How this differs from an outs list

A not-included list names photography, extra pages, a second platform. A short out of scope clause is the rule: the fee covers listed work only. The revision paragraph is the count. Rounds are the most common extra that still sounds like the original job. Name the count or you will spend round five on a new thing.

This is not a change request. The paragraph is for before they sign. After they ask for round four, you price the extra. You do not rewrite the proposal. You send a change request. Keep this page. Point at it.

What not to write

  • "Reasonable rounds included." Reasonable is how extras get in.
  • "We'll revise until you're happy." That is unlimited. Unlimited is not a fixed fee.
  • "Minor tweaks are free." Minor is their word. The cap is yours.
  • A threat about breach or courts. You are bounding a small job, not dressing as a firm.

When the paragraph is not enough

If they already asked for the extra round, you are late for this page. Use That's Extra and send a priced change request. If they mixed "revision" with a new page, read the Extra page on revision rounds vs extra work after the ask. If the invoice is unpaid after delivery, that is Still Due, not a new cap. If the job is large, a real advisor can write the agreement. This page will not pretend to be that.
